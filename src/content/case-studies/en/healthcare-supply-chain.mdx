## Problem

A hospital system buys from many vendors, often through a group purchasing organization (GPO) that negotiates contract prices for many hospitals. The platform keeps all of it in one place, in this flow:

1. **Data load:** hospital ERP and distributor data arrives as EDI (810 invoices, 850 purchase orders), HL7 and flat files.
2. **Item master:** each product is normalized into one master record (vendor, catalog number, unit of measure, UNSPSC).
3. **Contracts:** purchases are matched to contract prices; rebates pay out when spend reaches tier thresholds; price discrepancies are reviewed and approved or rejected.
4. **Requisitions and purchasing:** request, approval, then a purchase order sent to the vendor by EDI or punch-out.
5. **Spend analytics:** spend, invoice and purchase-order history, and savings.

## Architecture

<Diagram />

Ruby 3.1 on Rails 2.3 LTS, PostgreSQL, ExtJS 3.4, delayed_job, and Solr, Elasticsearch and OpenSearch for search. There is one main database, one centralized database, and one database per hospital system. Newer services read those per-hospital schemas directly, so every schema change is coordinated across teams.

## What I built

- **Contract Rebate Management**, end to end: rebates and tiers on contracts, spend tracking against baselines per cycle, an impact check before a change is applied, and audit history written by PostgreSQL triggers.
- **Future price review:** a two-level grid to compare upcoming and current contract prices across facilities, with bulk approve and reject running as background jobs.
- **Contract archiving:** moves old contract data to S3 and ClickHouse in verified batches, so hospital databases stay small without losing history.
- **Procurement and data loads:** shared quantity limits (9,999 → 99,999), punch-out settings, and faster nightly data-load steps.

## Trade-offs

- **A 2009-era framework:** new code has to fit Rails 2.3 and RSpec 1.3 idioms.
- **Shared schemas:** every migration runs on every hospital database and is read by other services, so it must be idempotent, indexed and backward compatible.
