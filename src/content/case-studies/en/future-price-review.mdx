_A price discrepancy is the difference between a price a hospital will pay and the price its contract says it should pay._

## Problem

Group purchasing organizations and vendors send new contract prices ahead of time. Before a future price goes live, someone has to decide whether it is right. A wrong price that slips through is paid again on every purchase order at every facility that buys the item.

The review is hard because one item can have a different future price per facility and per contract, a hospital system can have several corporations, and a single contract update can touch thousands of items.

## Approach & alternatives considered

I built the workflow end to end on the core platform (Oct 2025 – Oct 2026):

1. **A two-level grid.** Each row is an item; its sub-rows are the facilities or contracts the price applies to. A GPO panel on the left narrows the list, and several corporations can be reviewed together. Sorting and paging run on the server so large lists stay fast.
2. **Find the risky prices first.** Filters for percentage-change thresholds, date ranges and GPO, plus Solr search that also matches part of a contract number.
3. **Decide in bulk.** Approve, reject or pre-reject a selection or everything that matches the filters. Each action runs as a background job.
4. **Apply the pricing rules on approval.** Approving applies the best-contract and non-linear pricing rules before the price goes live.
5. **Keep a record.** A reviewed-prices screen lists past decisions, with search and sort across the sub-levels.
6. **Apply on schedule.** A job runs once a day and applies the approved future prices to their items when they take effect.

## Data design

Pending future prices live in each hospital system's own database, grouped by item with one sub-row per facility or contract. Search runs on a Solr index so partial contract-number matches stay fast.

## Failure handling

Bulk decisions run as background jobs, so approving or rejecting thousands of prices never blocks the screen or times out the request.

## Security

Each hospital system's prices stay in its own database. Only admin users, or users with full contract access, can approve or reject.

## Results

- Price discrepancies are reviewed and resolved in bulk before they take effect, instead of after the wrong price has been paid.
- Approved prices are applied to items by a daily job, instead of being created and applied by hand on the day they take effect.
- Model and controller specs for each step of the workflow.
